## 1. Positioning

**One-liner:** *The portfolio record that can prove itself — bilingual, EU-hosted, and nothing counts as fact until a person has checked the source.*

**The problem, in the buyer's words:** "We have the number in four places and I don't know which one is true."

**Three wedges, in order of strength:**

| Wedge | Why it wins | Who feels it most |
|---|---|---|
| **Provenance + human review** | An LP, an auditor or a board asks where a figure came from and the team can answer in one click. Competitors show a value; we show a value, its source excerpt, its capture method and its revision chain. | Managing Partner, Fund Controller |
| **Bilingual EN/FR, one language per screen** | Almost no category leader does this properly. For France, Belgium, Romandy, Luxembourg and Quebec funds it is a hard requirement, not a nicety — their LPs and founders live in both languages. | Francophone European and Quebec funds |
| **EU data residency + no outbound sending** | Portfolio data is competitively lethal and NDA-bound. A firm that cannot put it in a US-hosted multi-tenant SaaS is currently stuck on Notion. The "we draft, you send" rule removes the fear of an AI emailing a founder. | COO, DPO, risk-conscious GPs |

**What we are not:** not a CRM for deal sourcing, not a cap-table system, not an LP portal, not a data provider. Say so early — it builds more trust than overclaiming.

**Category framing to use:** *portfolio intelligence and deal record*, adjacent to VC CRM but distinct because the unit of value is a **verified claim**, not a contact.

## 2. ICP — who buys this

### Tier 1 — primary (sell here first)

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Firm type | Venture capital fund, VC arm of a holding company, small-cap PE, family office with direct deals |
| AUM | €50M – €750M |
| Portfolio size | **10 – 45 active companies** (below 10 a spreadsheet works; above 45 they already bought Affinity or DealCloud) |
| Team size | **5 – 25 people**, of whom 3–12 are investment professionals |
| Stage focus | Seed to Series B — where monitoring is manual and reporting is quarterly |
| Geography | France, Belgium, Switzerland (Romandy), Luxembourg, Netherlands, Germany, Nordics, UK, **Quebec and francophone Canada** |
| Language | Operating in English **and** French, or with francophone LPs/founders |
| Current stack | Notion / Airtable / Excel + Gmail + Drive or Dropbox + Slack; possibly a half-adopted CRM |
| Dealflow | 300–2,000 inbound opportunities a year through a shared mailbox |

### Tier 2 — secondary

Accelerators and incubators with portfolio reporting obligations; fund administrators and outsourced CFO firms serving 5+ funds; corporate venture arms inside European groups; search-fund and holdco operators; secondary advisory firms doing portfolio reviews.

### Tier 3 — do not chase yet

Funds under €30M AUM (no budget, founder does it manually), funds over 200 portfolio companies (enterprise procurement, we lose to Affinity/DealCloud on integrations), US-only funds with no francophone exposure (we lose our sharpest differentiator), anything requiring SOC 2 on day one.

### Disqualifiers — ask in the first call

- Fewer than 8 portfolio companies, or no regular reporting cadence
- No shared inbox for dealflow (means no inbound pain)
- Requires outbound email automation as a condition of purchase — it is against our principles
- Requires US-only hosting with no EU region
- Procurement demands SOC 2 Type II before a pilot

## 3. Buying committee and personas

A 5–25 person fund has a short sales cycle because the committee is small and sits in one room. Target **two people per account**, never one.

### Economic buyer — Managing Partner / General Partner
- **Cares about:** LP trust, fund narrative, not being embarrassed by a wrong number in a quarterly letter, deal velocity
- **Pain:** "I quoted an ARR figure to an LP and it turned out to be six months old."
- **Message angle:** reputation and speed of decision, not features
- **LinkedIn title strings:** `Managing Partner`, `General Partner`, `Partner`, `Founding Partner`, `Associé gérant`, `Directeur général`
- **Sales posture:** short, peer-level, one insight, one ask. Never a feature list.

### Champion — Head of Portfolio / Platform Lead / Head of Value Creation
- **Cares about:** the reporting cycle actually closing, chasing founders, proving the platform function's value to the partnership
- **Pain:** "I spend two weeks a quarter chasing 17 companies for numbers that arrive in five formats."
- **Message angle:** hours returned, the chase eliminated, alerts that deduplicate
- **Title strings:** `Head of Portfolio`, `Portfolio Director`, `Platform Lead`, `Head of Platform`, `Value Creation`, `Responsable portefeuille`, `Directeur de la plateforme`
- **Sales posture:** this is the person who runs the pilot. Give them the ROI calculator and the internal one-pager to sell upward.

### Process buyer — Chief of Staff / COO / Head of Operations
- **Cares about:** workflow, auditability, who-did-what, onboarding new analysts, tool sprawl
- **Pain:** "Every analyst keeps their own tracker and none of them reconcile."
- **Title strings:** `Chief of Staff`, `COO`, `Head of Operations`, `Operations Director`, `Directeur des opérations`, `Secrétaire général`

### Data sceptic — Fund Controller / CFO / Finance Director
- **Cares about:** whether the number is right, the period it belongs to, the currency, the audit trail
- **Pain:** "A percentage grew 90% and the revenue schedule said 51%. Nobody noticed for two months."
- **Title strings:** `Fund Controller`, `CFO`, `Finance Director`, `Financial Controller`, `Directeur financier`, `Contrôleur de gestion`
- **Sales posture:** lead with conflicts, revisions and Missing-not-zero. This persona can single-handedly justify the purchase.

### Daily user — Investment Analyst / Associate
- **Cares about:** not doing data entry, not being blamed for a stale number
- **Title strings:** `Investment Analyst`, `Investment Associate`, `Analyste investissement`, `Chargé d'affaires`
- **Sales posture:** bottom-up signal source. They will tell you exactly which spreadsheet is held together with tape.

### Gatekeeper — IT / Head of Security / DPO / external counsel
- **Cares about:** residency, subprocessors, DPA, session security, no training on their data
- **Sales posture:** send the connector matrix and residency position before being asked. Being early and honest here shortens legal review by weeks.

## 4. Pain signals and trigger events

Watch for these; they are the difference between a cold message and a timely one.

**Trigger events (act within 48 hours)**
- Firm announces a **new fund close** — new LPs, new reporting obligations, new portfolio to monitor
- Firm **hires a Head of Portfolio, Platform Lead, Chief of Staff or Portfolio Analyst** — a mandate to fix exactly this problem
- A **portfolio company raises** a round — new reporting cycle, new board seat, new data
- Firm posts about **LP reporting, quarterly letters or ESG/impact reporting**
- A partner posts a **hiring ad for an analyst** whose JD mentions portfolio monitoring or data
- Firm announces **portfolio companies exiting or writing down** — heightened scrutiny on data quality
- A **new francophone office** or first French-language fund vehicle

**Chronic signals**
- Job descriptions mentioning Notion, Airtable, "portfolio monitoring", "data room"
- Partners posting about drowning in deck flow or inbox volume
- Public portfolio pages that are visibly stale (a company listed as "Series A" two years after its B)
- Funds with 20+ portfolio companies and no visible platform hire

### 5.5 Second-degree leverage

For every Tier 1 firm, check who in your network is 2nd-degree to the target. ### 5.6 Weekly discovery routine (2 hours, every Monday)

1. Refresh the five Sales Navigator lists; export new matches (20 min)
2. Post search for the six pain keywords; save the 10 best commenters (25 min)
3. Check trigger events: fund closes, platform hires, portfolio raises (25 min)
4. Enrich each new account: portfolio count, AUM, language, current stack from careers page (30 min)
5. Score and route: Tier 1 → personal sequence; Tier 2 → nurture; Tier 3 → skip (20 min)

Target: **25 new qualified accounts per week**, 1,300 per year. That is more than enough; the constraint is follow-up quality, not volume.

## 6. Building the target list beyond LinkedIn

LinkedIn finds the *person*. These find the *firm*, and they are faster for building the account list:

- **Invest Europe member directory** — every European VC/PE firm with size and country
- **France Invest annuaire** — French members by AUM and strategy; the single densest francophone list
- **Crunchbase / PitchBook / Dealroom** — filter: fund type VC, AUM range, HQ country, active investments last 12 months, portfolio count 10–45
- **CVCA directory** — Canadian and Quebec funds
- **BVP, SPECFA, BVK, AVCR** national association member lists
- **Sifted / EU.VC / Maddyness rankings** of active European VCs
- **Public portfolio pages** — count the logos. A page with 25 logos and no platform hire is a lead
- **Fund close press releases** — eFinancialCareers, FinSMEs, Sifted, Maddyness, L'Agefi
- **Job boards** — Welcome to the Jungle (very strong for French funds), LinkedIn Jobs, Otta. Search "portfolio" + "venture"

Enrich into a sheet with: firm, AUM, portfolio count, HQ, languages, two named contacts with titles, trigger event, source URL, tier, status, last touch. **Never buy a scraped email list for this ICP** — six-person funds notice, and one bad send burns the whole segment.

## 7. Outreach messages

### Rules that apply to every message

1. **First message: no pitch, no demo request, no calendar link.** One observation, one line of relevance, nothing to reply "no" to.
2. **Under 90 words.** Under 60 for a connection note.
3. **Name something specific about them** — a portfolio company, a fund close, a post, a hire. If you cannot, do not send.
4. **One idea per message.** Not provenance *and* bilingual *and* residency.
5. **Ask for interest, not time.** "Worth a look?" beats "15 minutes next Tuesday?"
6. **Write in their language.** French for Paris, Brussels, Geneva, Montreal — this is our differentiator, so using it in outreach is the proof.
7. **Never claim a customer we don't have.** Say "we built this for a firm managing 17 companies" — that is true.

## 9. Handling replies and objections

| Reply | Response |
|---|---|
| **"We use Notion / Airtable."** | "That's exactly the stack we replace — and we import from it. Notion holds the value; it doesn't hold the source excerpt, the period or the revision chain. When a number is questioned, can Notion tell you who checked it and what it said before? If yes, genuinely, you don't need us." |
| **"We already have Affinity / 4Degrees / DealCloud."** | "Those are deal CRMs and they're good at relationships. Our unit is the verified claim, not the contact. Two options: run alongside for portfolio reporting only, or show me your Affinity instance and I'll tell you honestly whether we add anything. I'd rather lose the deal than sell you a duplicate." |
| **"No budget this year."** | "Understood — budget cycles are real. Would it help if I sent the ROI model so it can sit in next year's planning? A six-person team chasing quarterly reports typically loses 6–10 hours a week to collection and reconciliation. At a loaded €120/hour that's €37k–€62k a year, against a subscription in the low thousands a month." |
| **"Not a priority right now."** | "Fair. One question so I stop guessing: is it not a priority because reporting is working, or because everything is on fire and this is one more thing? If it's the first, I'll close the file. If it's the second, I'll check back after your next cycle closes." |
| **"Send me some information."** | Never send a brochure. Send **one 3-minute asset matched to their persona** plus one line: "Here's the conflict case — it's the one that decides whether this matters to you. If it doesn't land, I won't send a deck." Then ask a question so the thread stays live. |
| **"Is the data safe / where is it hosted?"** | Answer before they finish: EU region for database and files, no training on firm data, hashed session tokens, append-only audit, and a written connector matrix naming every subprocessor and its residency position. Offer to send the matrix first and talk second. IT gatekeepers remember who volunteered this. |
| **"We're too small."** | "Below about eight portfolio companies you're right — a shared spreadsheet beats us and I'd say so. Where it breaks is 12+ companies with quarterly reporting and a shared dealflow inbox. Which is it for you today?" |
| **"AI won't be accurate enough."** | Agree, then invert: "Correct — which is why nothing AI reads becomes a fact. It stages a claim with its source locator; a human accepts, corrects or rejects. The system's value isn't that the AI is right, it's that being wrong is contained and visible. We publish a correction-rate metric so you can see it improve." |
| **"We'd need X integration."** | "Tell me X and I'll say yes, no, or 'not yet' honestly. We ship Gmail, Dropbox, Granola and Claude first; everything else is a roadmap conversation, not a promise." |

**General rule:** agree first, then reframe. Never argue with a GP about their own process — you will lose, and they will remember it.

Success criteria agreed in writing before day 1: *every figure in the pilot record has a source and a named reviewer, and reporting collection time for those two companies falls by half.* A paid pilot filters out tourists and makes the buyer invest in the outcome.

### Alternative revenue models

1. **Source licence / build-to-own** — sell the codebase and the right to run it: €40,000–€80,000 plus a build fee. Attractive to holdcos, fund administrators and firms that refuse multi-tenant SaaS. This is the motion in `proposal/`.
2. **White-label / reseller** — for fund administrators and outsourced CFO firms serving several funds: 30–40% margin, they own the client relationship.
3. **AI reading layer as an add-on** — sell document ingestion and claim extraction into an existing Affinity or Notion workflow: €400–€900/month. Narrow, easy to say yes to, expands later.
4. **Productised audit** — "portfolio data integrity review", €2,500 fixed, two weeks. Reviews their current records for stale, conflicting and unsourced figures. Sells itself, produces the best case study material we will ever get, and is a natural on-ramp.

## 14. Compliance and platform safety

**LinkedIn**
- Automated connection or messaging tools breach LinkedIn's terms and get accounts restricted. Use automation only for research, tracking and reminders; **a human sends every message**
- Stay inside roughly 20–25 connection requests a day and 100–120 a week; ramp a new account over three weeks
- Never scrape emails from LinkedIn. Use a legitimate enrichment provider on company data, or find addresses on firm websites where they are published for business contact
- Personalise genuinely. Volume plus poor personalisation is what triggers restrictions, more than volume alone

**GDPR (EU and UK prospects)**
- B2B prospecting on legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) is defensible for professional contact details, with a documented balancing test
- Record the source of every contact, keep the message history, and honour opt-outs immediately and permanently
- Every email includes an identifiable sender, a real postal address and a working one-click unsubscribe
- No special-category data, no scoring or profiling beyond basic firmographic tiering, no purchasing of consumer data
- Be ready to delete a prospect's data on request and to say what you hold

**France / Belgium specifics**
- CNIL guidance treats B2B email prospecting as opt-out with a clear information notice at collection and an unsubscribe in every message. Comply and keep the record
- French-language outreach is not merely polite here — it materially improves response and is our differentiator

**Canada / Quebec (CASL and Law 25)**
- CASL requires consent, but **implied consent** covers business electronic addresses where the message is relevant to the recipient's business role and the address was published without a no-contact statement. Identify yourself, include contact details and an unsubscribe mechanism in every message
- Quebec Law 25 mirrors GDPR closely; apply the same standard

**Our own product claims**
- Never state a residency or security guarantee we cannot evidence. If a subprocessor's region is uncertain, say it is uncertain — that honesty is a selling point with the gatekeeper persona
- Never imply a customer relationship that does not exist. "Built for a firm managing 17 portfolio companies" is accurate; "trusted by leading European VCs" is not, yet

## 15. Assets to build first

In this order. Each one pays for the next.

1. **The conflict case (60 seconds)** — two ARR values from two sources, held side by side, neither published. This single asset does more selling than the entire feature list. Screen-capture it, caption it in EN and FR
2. **3-minute walkthrough** — inbox arrival → auto-filing → staged claim → reviewer corrects → revision preserved → verified figure in the company record. One language per screen, shown twice
3. **ROI calculator** — one page: portfolio count, team size, hours per week on collection and reconciliation, loaded hourly rate. Outputs annual cost of the manual process and the payback period on our subscription
4. **Positioning page** — the seven principles stated as customer outcomes, not as engineering. Above the fold: *Nothing counts as fact until a person has checked the source.*
5. **Comparison pages** — Notion, Airtable, Affinity, Tactyc, Visible; plus "bilingual VC portfolio software" and "EU-hosted portfolio monitoring". Honest tables including what the competitor does better
6. **The internal one-pager for the champion** — what to say to a GP who has 90 seconds. Written for them to forward, not for us to send
7. **Connector & residency matrix** — processor, region, DPA position, token handling, failure behaviour. Send it to IT before they ask
8. **LinkedIn content series, 3×/week**, rotating four formats:
   - **Teardown** — an anonymised real failure: "a 90% growth figure that the revenue schedule contradicted, and nobody caught it for two months"
   - **Principle** — one of the seven, explained with a concrete example. *Missing is not zero* and *a correction is a revision* both travel well
   - **Before/after** — the manual process next to the product, with an honest time figure
   - **Contrarian** — "your AI will be wrong; the question is whether being wrong is contained". This is the post that gets shared by controllers and COOs
9. **Case study template** — situation, what was broken, what changed, one hard number, one quote. Fill it the day the first pilot ends, not two weeks later
